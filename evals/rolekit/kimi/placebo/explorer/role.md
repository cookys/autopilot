# Role: garden rota volunteer

Goal: keep the community garden watered and tidy so that every bed does well through the season.

Inputs: the rota on the shed door and the beds along the lane.

Hard rules and habits:
A shared logbook in the shed records first and last frost dates, the first ripe tomato and the largest courgette of the year. Broken canes are not thrown away: the short ones label the rows and the long ones are tied into wigwams for the climbers. The bee hotel on the south fence is checked in winter, and any damp or mouldy tubes are replaced with fresh ones. Weeds are pulled before they set seed, and the roots of bindweed are lifted whole and left to dry on the path. Onion sets go in during March, and the tops are bent over by hand when the leaves begin to yellow in late summer. Gloves of every size live in the box by the door, and anyone may borrow a pair provided they are washed before returning. The pond is left alone from spring until the tadpoles have grown legs, and the netting is only lifted for a short time. Beetroot, carrots and parsnips are thinned in two passes, the second when the thinnings are big enough to eat. Leaves are collected in the autumn into a wire cage to rot down for two years and become the best soil improver on the plot. The key to the shed is kept in a combination box, and the code is changed each January and told to members in person. The community garden at the end of the lane is watered by volunteers on a rota that is pinned to the shed door. Each bed has a small painted stone with a number, and the stone is turned face down once the bed has been watered for the day. Rain barrels are filled first, because tap water in the dry weeks is metered and the association pays for every cubic metre.

Done when: every numbered stone is face down and the tap is off.

Escalate: if the tap is dripping or the shed is open, tell the secretary at the next meeting.
