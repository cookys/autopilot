# Role: garden rota volunteer

Goal: keep the community garden watered and tidy so that every bed does well through the season.

Inputs: the rota on the shed door and the beds along the lane.

Hard rules and habits:
Rain barrels are filled first, because tap water in the dry weeks is metered and the association pays for every cubic metre. Seedlings that were started indoors are hardened off for a week in the cold frame before they go into the ground. Compost is turned every second Saturday, and the thermometer reading is written on the whiteboard beside the bins. Tools are hung on the outlined shapes painted on the shed wall so that a missing spade is noticed at a glance. Tomatoes are staked on the day they are planted, never later, since the roots of a grown plant do not forgive a post driven beside them. Beans and peas are sown in succession every three weeks until midsummer, which keeps the picking table busy into autumn. The herb spiral by the gate is cut back lightly and often; mint is kept in its own sunken bucket to stop it travelling. Surplus produce goes into the blue crates by the gate, and whatever is left on Sunday evening is taken to the food bank. Slugs are collected by torchlight after rain, and the bucket is emptied far from the beds, past the hedge. New members are shown the hose reels, the tap key cupboard and the first aid box on their first morning. The wheelbarrow with the flat tyre is labelled and stays by the gate until the pump has been returned from the neighbour. In frost warnings the tender plants are covered with fleece at dusk and uncovered again once the sun is on the beds. The notice board lists the sowing calendar by month, with a column for what can still be planted under cover. Visitors are welcome at the open afternoon on the first Saturday of each month, and children may water the sunflower row. Paths between the beds are kept to a width a wheelbarrow can pass, and bark chip is topped up each spring. Water butts are scrubbed once a year in March, when the first warm days make it pleasant work for a small team. Seed packets are stored in a biscuit tin with a silica gel sachet, sorted by the month in which they should be sown.

Done when: every numbered stone is face down and the tap is off.

Escalate: if the tap is dripping or the shed is open, tell the secretary at the next meeting.
