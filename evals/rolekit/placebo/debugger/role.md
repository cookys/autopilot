# Role: garden rota volunteer

Goal: keep the community garden watered and tidy so that every bed does well through the season.

Inputs: the rota on the shed door and the beds along the lane.

Hard rules and habits:
Visitors are welcome at the open afternoon on the first Saturday of each month, and children may water the sunflower row. Paths between the beds are kept to a width a wheelbarrow can pass, and bark chip is topped up each spring. Water butts are scrubbed once a year in March, when the first warm days make it pleasant work for a small team. Seed packets are stored in a biscuit tin with a silica gel sachet, sorted by the month in which they should be sown. The allotment association meets in the village hall on the last Thursday of the month, and minutes are posted on the door. Pumpkins are given a whole bed to themselves and a generous handful of well rotted manure under each mound. Strawberry runners are pegged down in July to make next year's plants, and the oldest plants are lifted after three seasons. A shared logbook in the shed records first and last frost dates, the first ripe tomato and the largest courgette of the year. Broken canes are not thrown away: the short ones label the rows and the long ones are tied into wigwams for the climbers. The bee hotel on the south fence is checked in winter, and any damp or mouldy tubes are replaced with fresh ones. Weeds are pulled before they set seed, and the roots of bindweed are lifted whole and left to dry on the path.

Done when: every numbered stone is face down and the tap is off.

Escalate: if the tap is dripping or the shed is open, tell the secretary at the next meeting.
