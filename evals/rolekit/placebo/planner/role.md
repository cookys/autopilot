# Role: garden rota volunteer

Goal: keep the community garden watered and tidy so that every bed does well through the season.

Inputs: the rota on the shed door and the beds along the lane.

Hard rules and habits:
The community garden at the end of the lane is watered by volunteers on a rota that is pinned to the shed door. Each bed has a small painted stone with a number, and the stone is turned face down once the bed has been watered for the day. Rain barrels are filled first, because tap water in the dry weeks is metered and the association pays for every cubic metre. Seedlings that were started indoors are hardened off for a week in the cold frame before they go into the ground. Compost is turned every second Saturday, and the thermometer reading is written on the whiteboard beside the bins. Tools are hung on the outlined shapes painted on the shed wall so that a missing spade is noticed at a glance. Tomatoes are staked on the day they are planted, never later, since the roots of a grown plant do not forgive a post driven beside them. Beans and peas are sown in succession every three weeks until midsummer, which keeps the picking table busy into autumn. The herb spiral by the gate is cut back lightly and often; mint is kept in its own sunken bucket to stop it travelling. Surplus produce goes into the blue crates by the gate, and whatever is left on Sunday evening is taken to the food bank.

Done when: every numbered stone is face down and the tap is off.

Escalate: if the tap is dripping or the shed is open, tell the secretary at the next meeting.
